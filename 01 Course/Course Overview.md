## Goal
Explore data-driven studies of security, privacy and undesirable behaviors. Do a limited (few studies) but in-depth literature review, then develop your own research idea and take it as far as you can.

### Reading studies critically
- How is the data collected? Is it representative? Are there significant sampling biases?
- Is the data publicly available?
- Is there enough information to replicate the results or do follow-up research?
- What is the analysis methodology? Are the findings robust?
- What are the takeaways?

### Own research idea
- What problem do you want to tackle? Is data available?
- Which method of analysis fits?

## Deliverables
### Seminar paper
- About 15 pages, using a common LaTeX or Word template
- About 10–12 technical references, plus additional non-technical ones
- References must be integrated and discussed, not just listed
- Research idea section: at least 2 pages (no upper limit)
- Appendix: data artifacts, analysis artifacts, code
- Focus on material that is core to your idea, not LLM output

### Seminar talk
- 20 min per person plus discussion (30 min slot in total)

### Participation
- Attendance and contributions to seminar discussions
- Feedback on peers' contributions

## Rules
- Don't write or present something uninteresting.
- No plagiarism. Cite formal and informal sources properly; email the instructor if unsure.
- **LLMs: fully disclose any use of AI-based tools in the final report.**

## Methodologies
- **Data:** measurement studies (company behavior, user data); analysis of available data (e.g., business data)
- **Experiments/surveys:** small-scale data collection and user studies; psychological phenomena, (bounded) rational economic behavior, perceptions/valuations
- **Policy:** impact of regulation or regulatory proposals; feasibility of self-regulation (e.g., Do-Not-Track, GDPR, CCPA)
- **System design:** incentive-compatible protocols and systems
- **Theory:** mathematical modeling/optimization, game theory
